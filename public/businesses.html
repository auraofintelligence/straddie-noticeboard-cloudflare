For Straddie businesses

Publish a local update, host a community screen or do both. Plan what your business could support for a possible island-wide December–January school-holiday launch and who would look after it year-round.

The network and its convenor, Ready SETCO, are proposals. Businesses in Dunwich, Amity Point and Point Lookout could prepare in parallel, alongside community venues, ferry or bus operators and bus stop owners that choose to take part.

A changed opening time, a local event, a workshop or a call for help can start as one short notice. Agree which subjects the business may publish and who can confirm each item.

Pick a public source, such as your own opening-hours or event page. Give the notice a take-down date and choose the town or audience it serves.

Host a screen

  1. Agree on a site
  2. Connect the player
  3. Test the display

Start with permission from the venue owner or operator. Try a wide TV preview on an existing display and decide what people can read from the queue, footpath or ferry cabin.

Use a supported browser or a signage player. Agree which approved topics and towns will appear, who can pause the display and where to put printed information if the screen goes dark. Check text size, glare and access from a seated position.

For an onboard bus screen, get agreement from the operator and vehicle owner and use the wide format on an approved passenger display. At a high-traffic stop, get agreement from the site owner, stop asset owner and transport operator. Choose wide for a passive screen or kiosk for interactive use. Passenger service, accessibility and safety information takes priority at both.

Take one notice from draft to display

  1. Name the stewards. Decide which role owns the facts, which role checks the draft and who controls the display. Agree a roster and backup cover for holiday hours and ongoing operation.
  2. Send a first draft for review. Start with one public notice. Share the draft with the person who can confirm the wording, source, permissions and take-down time.
  3. Rehearse the handover. The reviewer would give the screen host the approved copy, its version and take-down time. Test the preview on the actual display. Photograph the screen during the rehearsal only if taking the photo is permitted.
  4. Try a correction and removal. Ask each host that received the draft version to verify the correction on their own display. Check that the old copy comes down by its expiry, then tell the reviewer which screens and channels are up to date.

Permissions and costs

Take part by choice. Agree equipment ownership, venue hours, approved places and content, who can update the display, and who removes or transfers it if the venue withdraws. Confirm permission for the screen location, power, network access, photos and any branding.

Separate capital funding for accessible equipment and installation from ongoing funding for steward shifts, connectivity, software, inspection and repairs. Include replacement batteries, screens and spare parts in the longer-term budget.
