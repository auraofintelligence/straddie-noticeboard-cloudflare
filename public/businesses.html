For Straddie businesses

Publish a local update, host a community screen or do both. Use this guide to plan one small trial and decide who would look after it.

The network and its convenor, Ready SETCO, are proposals. This prototype offers sample notices, screen previews and tools you can use to plan a trial.

Publish notices

  1. Write a draft
  2. Get approval
  3. Share the notice

A changed opening time, a local event, a workshop or a call for help can start as one short notice. Agree which subjects the business may publish and who can confirm each item.

Pick a public source, such as your own opening-hours or event page. Give the notice a take-down date and choose the town or audience it serves.

Host a screen

  1. Agree on a site
  2. Connect the player
  3. Test the display

Start with permission from the venue owner or operator. Try a wide TV preview on an existing display and decide what people can read from the queue, footpath or ferry cabin.

Use a supported browser or a signage player. Agree which approved topics and towns will appear, who can pause the display and where to put printed information if the screen goes dark.

Doing both? Record the publisher and host responsibilities in one plan, including who covers each job when the usual person is away.

Take one notice from draft to display

  1. Name the stewards. Decide which role owns the facts, which role checks the draft and who controls the display. Agree a backup for days the usual person is away.
  2. Send a first draft for review. Start with one public notice. Share the draft with the person who can confirm the wording, source, permissions and take-down time.
  3. Rehearse the handover. The reviewer would give the screen host the approved copy, its version and take-down time. Test the preview on the actual display. Photograph the screen during the rehearsal only if taking the photo is permitted.
  4. Try a correction and removal. Ask the host to switch to an updated version and verify the change on the screen. Check that the old copy comes down by its expiry, and agree where readers can go for the corrected information.

Permissions and costs

Take part by choice. Agree the venue’s hours, who can update the display and how the venue could withdraw. Confirm permission for the screen location, power, network access, photos and any branding.

Price the player, mount or other installation, connectivity, staff time and ongoing support. For a touch kiosk, include safe placement, tested battery runtime and backup supplies.
