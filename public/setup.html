Choose a screen

Ask the venue owner or ferry operator first. Agree where the screen goes and who looks after it. Check that people can read it where they stand or sit, including in sunlight or when the venue is busy.

Phone or tablet

Open the sample board in a browser. For a venue trial, put the link or a QR code at the counter so people can read the details on their own phone. Print a short address alongside the code.

Networked TV

Try the wall view in a club, shop window or ferry where the operator agrees. Use the TV’s browser if it displays the preview reliably, or plug in a supported signage player or small computer over HDMI. Connect it to the host’s approved network.

Battery-powered touchscreen

Choose a touch display and computer that can run on their own tested battery supply. Give people simple controls to browse notices, find a place on a saved map and return to the start. Keep the software and approved content on the device.

Portable information screen

Use a charged tablet or a small portable display and player for events, pop-up venues or a backup point. Load the local pack before moving it. Give the person taking it a charger and a plain-language handover.

Put in a notice

  1. Write it in plain language

    Say what is happening, who it’s for, where it is and what people can do next. Add the public source, publisher, town, relevant dates and when the notice should come down. Keep the main message short enough to read on a TV.

  2. Send the draft for review

    The draft tool saves work in your browser. Download the plain-text draft and share it with the publisher or the person who would review it. Confirm the facts, source, permissions, photo credits, expiry and places where it can appear. Leave private details out and seek permission from the relevant custodians for cultural material.

  3. Approve and keep it current

    In a pilot, a named reviewer would release the approved copy to participating venues. Use the same title, source and take-down time on each screen. Log the last check, have a way to correct or withdraw it, and confirm that the old copy has been removed.

Council and official information

Show official warnings in the issuer’s own words with the affected area, issue time and source. When an issuer updates or cancels a message, record the time and revision, replace the older message and check which screens have received the change. Council’s warning and alert channels

Before putting official information on the screens, agree who can relay it, how to reach the issuer, and who can keep the service running after hours. Use the published local arrangements when defining those responsibilities. Redland City Local Disaster Management Plan (PDF)

Plan for outages

Power and internet can fail separately. A screen can be on battery and still online, or plugged in and offline. A pilot should test both.

Keep an approved local copy

Save a small pack with the player, text notices, readable maps, source labels and practical guides. Test opening the pack from a full restart with no internet. Keep the last complete approved pack until its replacement has loaded and been checked.

Show when it was checked

When offline, display “Offline · last verified” with the date, time and source in Queensland local time. Hide expired routine notices. For a warning with uncertain status, show its original issue time and “Current status unavailable”. If the clock is unreliable, use standing information and mark current checks as unavailable.

Use a low-power fallback

Keep readable text, sources and simple directions when images or video fail. Measure battery runtime with the display and player running at the venue’s normal brightness. Include any router required for the local service in the test. Agree when to switch to essential information, when to recharge and who will do it.

Use a second way to communicate

Agree how the steward can check an update when the main link fails. Keep an approved list of issuer and venue contacts with the saved pack. When all links are down, show where people can ask in person or find printed information. After reconnection, check for withdrawals and corrections before restoring the normal display.

Keep everyday information useful

Keep dated maps, text directions, public facility information and short guides on the device. Give people a way to learn how to check a forecast or read a warning. Mark an evacuation centre as open only when the relevant authority confirms it has been activated. Council’s Minjerribah disaster profile

Track which screens need attention

A screen that hasn’t checked in may still be showing an older copy. The steward should record its last contact and tell the host to check what’s actually on screen. Log each correction as confirmed on the display when the host or player can verify it.

Run a first pilot

Start indoors with one willing host, one screen and a handful of clearly labelled sample notices.

  1. Name the people. Agree who hosts the device, who reviews notices and who covers each role if someone is away.
  2. Write down the setup. Record the screen name and location, power and network needs, how to start the player and where the saved pack is kept.
  3. Walk through a normal day. Try a draft, check it, display it and remove it. Ask people to read it from where they would normally stand and try the touchscreen controls without help.
  4. Practise a disruption. In an agreed test, simulate lost internet and separately test running on battery. Restart the player offline, check dates and text fallback, and measure how long essential information stays available. Confirm what returns when the connection is restored.
  5. Record the limits. Before the test, agree how long the screen needs to run on battery and how quickly it should show text after restart. Measure both. Time how long a correction takes to reach the screen after reconnection and note whether visitors can find and read the source. Assign each fault to a named person before expanding the trial.

If the pilot aims to cover multi-day outages, run a longer exercise that includes recharging, spare equipment, source checks and relief staffing.

Steward handover

Leave a dated note wherever the next steward can find it, including offline.

  • Screen name, host and backup contact.
  • Power and connection state, battery level and next check.
  • Which saved pack is installed and when sources were last verified.
  • Corrections still to apply and venues that haven’t confirmed them.
  • Where to find the offline pack, restart instructions and printed fallback.
