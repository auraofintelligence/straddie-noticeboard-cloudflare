How the network would work

Each notice would have one record with its source, permissions and expiry. The web board, standard displays and touchscreens would all use that record so people can check the source and see when the information changes.

The proposed first operating season would aim for a December–January school-holiday launch, with publishers and candidate hosts in Dunwich, Amity Point and Point Lookout preparing at the same time. Dates and coverage would depend on the venues, people and funding in place. Island launch plan

From a local tip to a checked notice

  1. Gather the information

    Start with an event detail, a question, an interview, a photo or an official source. Name the person or group who can confirm the details.

  2. Verify the source

    Check the issuer, place, dates and affected audience. For bus service notices, verify routes, directions, affected stops and effective times against the operator’s public update. Record who can correct it.

  3. Prepare the notice

    Write the public details in plain language. Choose the web board, TVs and standard displays, or touchscreen kiosks. Check photo credits and keep permission records with the people responsible for them.

    Try the draft tool

  4. Approve and publish

    A named reviewer checks the wording, confirms the publisher’s outlet choices and agrees a take-down time. Check permission with the operator or host before using a bus or stop screen. The steward records the approved choices in the public feed.

  5. Adapt for each screen

    Use a short, readable headline on venue TVs, onboard buses and standard displays at busy stops. Phones can link to the source. Interactive kiosks at a venue or stop can offer a map or more detail.

    Try a screen preview

  6. Correct or take it down

    Log the correction against the original notice and send it to the same channels across towns. Have each intended venue confirm the replacement. Track displays that are offline separately and expire routine notices on schedule.

What a notice needs

Details visitors can use

A short title, place, dates, who it’s for and what someone can do next. Show the publisher, a public source or contact and when it was last checked.

Information for the reviewer and host

Record the person responsible, approved channels, credits and permissions, version and take-down time. A host needs to confirm which version is on screen and whom to call for a correction.

Who would be responsible?

The author or publisher
Provides the details, public source and permission to use the material. Supplies corrections when something changes.
Ready SETCO, the proposed convenor and moderator
Would help publishers, reviewers and screen hosts agree who can publish, how to cover review shifts and how to handle complaints and corrections across towns. Cultural material would still need permission from the people with authority to share it.
The screen host
Agrees what the venue will show, names the equipment owner and maintenance contact, keeps the screen usable and checks it after a correction or outage. Businesses could choose to publish, host, or do both.
Council, agencies and transport operators
Remain the source of their own official updates. A future relay would preserve the issuer’s wording, times and area, with an agreed way to check new versions and cancellations.
