Get the pilot ready

  1. Name the people and organisations

    Record the legal applicant, its authorised signatory and the person who will keep the accounts and submit the final report. If an eligible organisation agrees to administer the grant for the project, record that auspice in a signed agreement. Name a venue manager, an editor and a backup reviewer.

  2. Show the need and get permission

    Ask residents and local groups where they currently miss notices. Keep a short summary, two permission-cleared sample notices and letters describing what each participant has agreed to provide. Get written host approval for the screen, internet access, operating hours and equipment ownership.

  3. Price the work and confirm the rules

    Collect itemised quotes for the screen setup, workshop and review work. Confirm insurance, venue and landowner requirements, eligible costs, the approved start date and how the host would cover ongoing expenses.

  4. Run the trial and report back

    Install and check the display, run the workshop and trial the review process. Keep invoices, source and permission notes, attendance totals and a log of changes. Report what the grant paid for and what the pilot achieved.

In-kind contributions

Illustrative total of $1,300 for an existing TV ($400), workshop space ($600) and host support ($300). Confirm the contribution in writing and use the funder’s valuation rules.

After the trial

Allow for editing time, internet and power, hosting or backup, and repairs. For example, four hours of review a month at $60 an hour is $2,880 a year before other costs. Ask the host how this would be covered.

Decide if it should continue

Agree on targets with the host before the trial. Keep aggregate counts and first-hand feedback that can be recorded with permission.

  • Participation. Aim for six to ten learners and record how many finish a captioned, source-checked practice item.
  • Publishing. Aim to review and display 12 notices. Track time from draft to publication and whether expired items come down on time.
  • Access. Ask visitors whether they found an update they needed and could read it easily. Test the screen’s behaviour when the internet drops out.
  • Ongoing work. Record actual costs and weekly review time. Ask the host to decide who would own the screen and run the board after the pilot.

Sample project wording

We propose an eight-week trial of a shared community noticeboard on Minjerribah. An agreed host venue would display reviewed notices on an existing TV. A supervised workshop would help residents produce short, accessible updates. We would measure participation, how easily people can find current notices and what it takes for the host to keep the board running.

Add the confirmed applicant, host, location and local evidence before reusing this wording in an application.
