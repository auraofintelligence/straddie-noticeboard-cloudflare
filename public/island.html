How a notice could help at One Mile

At the real Yabby Street bus stop by One Mile Ferry Terminal, a display could point travellers to the 880 for Point Lookout or the 881 for Amity Point and show where to check for changes. With operator and site approval, an onboard screen could preview the transfer, a read-only display at the stop could show a short update, and a separate touchscreen could let people browse an island map and official links.

Read a weather display

A future public screen could show the source, place and timestamp beside each item. This demo links to the official pages for current conditions.

Forecast

A forecast describes what is expected over a stated period. Check the place, issue time and the hours the forecast covers.

Observations

Observations record what a station measured at a stated time. Check the station name and time, since wind and rain can differ across the island.

Rain radar

Read the time on each frame to see where rainfall echoes have moved. Pair the radar with the forecast and official warnings.

Future island transport · November 2026

What would the board show at a new pickup point?

Imagine a resident stepping off the ferry in Dunwich and looking for a connecting ride. If a locally agreed robotaxi pilot opened, a stop screen could show its operating area, accessible pickup point, hours and a phone number for help. If it paused, the same screen could direct people to the bus. Bookings and passenger details would stay with the operator.

Could Straddie go early?

Luke’s Moreton Bay proposal explores how local bus, taxi and community transport operators could help own and run new services around ferry connections. He proposes exploring an island pilot ahead of wider rollout. Australia’s transport ministers agreed on conditional deployment in selected locations from 2027. Selection of a Straddie route remains an open question.

Moreton Bay action plan Transport ministers’ decision (PDF)

Tesla’s Australian roadshow

Tesla listed its Cybercab for public display at Burleigh Heads in October 2026 and Adelaide in November. Those events let people see the vehicle. A local passenger service would need its own operator, approvals and published service details before the board could list a ride.

Tesla’s Burleigh event Tesla’s Adelaide event

Gold Coast air taxis

On 17 November 2026 SkyDrive announced an agreement with the City of Gold Coast to study electric air taxis. SkyDrive and Gold Coast Helitours aim for Australia’s first commercial electric air taxi service from 2028. The SD-05 design carries a pilot and two passengers, with a published range of 15 to 40 km. A proposed link to Straddie would need specific landing sites, a feasible route, local agreement and approvals.
