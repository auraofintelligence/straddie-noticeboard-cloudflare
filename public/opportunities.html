Work & volunteering

Find paid roles or ways to volunteer on Minjerribah. If you’ve already given time to a community activity, try a worksheet you can keep or share with the organiser.

Find opportunities

For work available now, check the employer’s or volunteer organisation’s listing and apply directly. The proposed noticeboard would also need local editors, screen technicians and event helpers. Those roles would depend on funding and agreements with island hosts.

Paid work

Search for 4183, Dunwich, Amity Point or Point Lookout. Try cafés, accommodation and ferry services. Check where each shift starts and ends, since a role serving Straddie could be based in Cleveland. Ask about pay and duties and check whether the first and last ferries suit the roster.

Volunteering

Ask the island museum in Dunwich about helping with community programmes, or ask Council’s Point Lookout Bushcare group about a working bee around Headland Park. Contact the group before you go to confirm the meeting point, what to bring and any induction or access arrangements.

At a community music evening, you could help set up, welcome people at the door or pack down. Ask the organiser what help they need.

Working holidays on Straddie

Travellers who spend a season working on the island bring languages, music and stories from home. Sharing a meal or joining an island club is a way to get to know the people you live and work alongside.

If your visa requires specified work, check the subclass, eligible industry and actual worksite postcode before counting shifts. Home Affairs lists 4183 for eligible tourism and hospitality work. The second-visa requirement changes from 88 to 120 days on 1 March 2027 for applicants who must complete specified work. Check the dates and rules that apply to you.

Join a local response team

To help with wildlife rescue, contact the island group listed by Council and ask about joining and training. If you find injured wildlife, follow the group’s reporting advice.

Rural fire brigades have support roles as well as firefighting roles. Find your local brigade through the Queensland Fire Department and ask how to apply and train for the role.

Community Hours (C-Hours)

The C-Hour proposal would recognise one checked hour of voluntary human contribution as one C-Hour, without a money value. Keep paid hours and wages separate. This worksheet helps you describe a voluntary session and calculate its length for a conversation with the activity organiser.

Work out a voluntary session

The worksheet runs in this browser. Use plain activity descriptions and role names. Leave out people’s names, contacts and private details.

Count time you freely gave during the activity. Keep breaks, paid time and machine time separate.

Private worksheet

Review the activity and duration yourself. If you both agree to share it, ask the organiser or the identified role to confirm the time. The worksheet is an unconfirmed draft and carries no credit.

75 minutes would be 1 hour and 15 minutes.
